3.3 Personal Safety

Please wear protection gear before operations to prevent electric shocks or burns.

Equipment operates in high voltage. Any accidental contact may result in a fatal shock hazard. It is essential to have at least two personnel presents during maintenance or operation for safety.

When the equipment is powered on, it is strictly prohibited to move, open, or dismantle the electric equipment container door or touch any internal components. Before performing maintenance operations, ensure the equipment has been safely powered off.

Avoid contact cords or operating equipment during storms.

3.4 Electrical Safety

After performing AC and DC double-side disconnect operations on the energy storage system, wait at least 15 minutes before opening the front door to perform maintenance, overhaul, or other functions on the energy storage system.

When the energy storage system is electrically connected for a test run or other operations, use high-quality measuring equipment with a range and accuracy that meets the site requirements to ensure that the electrical parameters meet the requirements. Ensure that measuring equipment is correctly connected and used according to standards to prevent arcs and other hazards.

Even if all external circuits are disconnected, high voltage may still exist between the positive and negative terminals of the energy storage battery pack. If measurements shall be conducted under charged conditions, use dedicated instruments, and ensure that only qualified professionals perform the tests.

If the energy storage system operates abnormally or requires maintenance or an, shut down the equipment and confirm that the energy storage system completely stops running before proceeding with any maintenance or repair work.

Grounding The following requirements apply to equipment that shall be grounded:

  • During installation, it shall be grounded first.
  • During removal, the ground wire shall be the last item disconnected.
  • Before operations, check the electrical connections to ensure that the equipment is reliably grounded. Operate the equipment without a grounding conductor is strictly prohibited.
  • The equipment shall remain permanently connected to a protective earth. Destruction of the grounding conductor is prohibited.

(HyperStrong uses TN-S protective earthing system).

3.5 Operational Safety

Read all equipment information and instructions carefully and completely before operation.

Before operation, please make sure that all cables are correctly connected. During operation, please pay attention to the normal operation of the equipment fan. Do not block the vent.

Do not place small metal devices such as crank pins, screws, etc. next to connectors, slots, sockets, or circuits to prevent short circuits.

If the equipment gets damaged, please do not repair it yourself. Contact HyperStrong to assign professional personnel to repair. Do not modify the internal equipment of the battery system privately to avoid danger.

3.6 Electrostatic Protection

Accumulation of static electricity can result in electric shocks, fire, explosion, failure and damage of electronic devices, and other safety hazards. The energy storage system contains circuit boards and other static-sensitive components.

To mitigate the risks associated with static electricity, operators shall implement static protection measures, minimize static electricity generation, accelerate the dissipation of static electricity, and neutralize static electricity.

The prevention methods include but are not limited

to:

  • During replacement, all equipment devices that have not yet been installed should be retained in bags with anti-static shielding, and temporarily removed equipment devices should be placed on foam pads with anti-static features.
  • Do not touch soldering points, pins, or exposed circuits.

EMC Related Cautions The system’s emission characteristics make it suitable for use in industrial areas, meaning Class A electromagnetic environment. If used in a residential environment, where Class B is typically required, this equipment may not provide adequate protection to radio-frequency communication services. User may need to implement mitigation measures, such as relocating or re-orienting the equipment.

Mains power quality should conform to the standards of a specialized industrial distribution network. For users requiring uninterrupted operation during or after power mains interruptions, it is recommended to connect the system's information storage components to an uninterruptible power supply (UPS) or a backup power source.

Some portable RF communications equipment such as high-power walkie-talkie may degrade the electromagnetic performance of the system. If the essential system performance is lost or impaired due to Electromagnetic disturbances, maintain a safe distance between high-power transmitters and the system, and contact customer service to address the issue.

  • When walking on top of the equipment, strictly follow the procedures for working at heights.
  • Wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) as required, including safety helmets, work clothing, safety shoes, and gloves.
  • When using lifting machinery, adhere to all relevant safety operation guidelines. Do not engage in improper lifting practices.
  • Protect the equipment during handling and storage. Prevent it from falling, tumbling, or being subjected to excessive pressure.

For those handling hazardous materials, training should include emergency response procedures for fire prevention, spill containment, and emergency contact protocols. They shall be familiar with the items being transported and understand the correct handling procedures.

3.8 Installation and Wiring Safety

  • During the entire mechanical and electrical installation process, strictly adhere to the relevant standards and requirements of the project location.
  • Electrical wiring for the system shall only be operational after inspection and verification. Ensure warning signs are prominently displayed during the process, such as "Personnel Working: Do Not Close the Gate" or "High-Voltage Danger.".

4 Transportation And Storage

4.1 Cautions

  • If the ESS fails to be transported and stored in accordance with the requirements specified in this manual, the warranty may be voided.

4.2 Type of Transportation

  • BESS containers can be transported by road, sea, and rail. With an integrated and user-friendly lifting design, ESS containers are easy to be handled during transportation. However, air transportation is not permitted, and specific guidelines for rail transportation are not available currently.
  • The BESS container can be transported domestically by truck.

NOTE: In most cases, when transported by truck, the total weight of the truck, including the BESS container, may exceed the limitations allowed by the general roadways. In such instances, an overweight permit may be required in the shipping region.

Figure 4-1 Schematic Diagram of BESS Container Transportation

4.3 Transportation Requirements

The equipment in the BESS has been installed and securely fixed inside the container before shipment, allowing the container to be lifted and transported as a single unit.

  • The applicable national safety procedures and regulations shall be complied with during the operating processes of loading, unloading, and transportation for BESS containers in the country where the project is located.
  • The BESS containers and all handling equipment used in the operating processes shall be properly maintained
  • All personnel involved in loading, unloading and bolting shall be trained accordingly, especially safety training.
  • The mechanical parameters such as external dimension, gross weight of each battery prefabrication pod shall be clarified and verified during the entire operating processes of loading, unloading, and transportation.

The following requirement shall be fulfilled prior to the transportation of BESS

containers:

  • All cabinet doors within the battery container shall be securely locked.
  • A suitable crane or lifting device shall be selected based on sitespecific conditions with adequate load capacity, boom length, and radius of rotation.
  • When lifting the ESS container, it is recommended to use a single crane.
  • Additional towing or traction equipment may be required when moving across inclined surfaces or slopes.
  • The intended route shall be cleared of obstructions such as overhanging trees, power lines, or other impediments.
  • The transportation and movement of ESS containers should be conducted under favourable weather conditions whenever practicable.
  • Warning signs or cordons shall be set up and formed to restrict access to the lifting and transportation area and prevent accidents.

When transported by land, ropes shall be used to secure the corner fittings on the top of the BESS container to the vehicle and ensure that the ESS container remains stable and the inclination angle is minimized during transportation.

4.4 Storage Requirements

  • In order to prevent possible condensation or the bottom soaking from rainwater in the rainy season. The BESS should be stored on higher place.
  • It is necessary to raise the container base to acclimatize site-specific conditions. The specific height should be reasonably determined based on the geological and meteorological conditions of the site.
  • Store the BESS on dry, flat, and stable ground with sufficient loadbearing capacity and free of vegetation.
  • Ensure the container doors and all internal equipment are securely locked.
  • Storage environment temperature: -35~60℃.
  • Long-term storage is not recommended because it may cause a decrease in battery capacity. Even when stored at the recommended storage temperature, irreversible capacity fading will still occur over time. The longer the system is stored, the greater of capacity will fade. Refer to the technical protocol for specific capacity fade rates.
  • The relative humidity should be between 0~95%, without condensation.
  • Protect the air inlet and outlet of the BESS to prevent rain water, sand, and dust from entering the container.
  • Perform periodic inspections.
  • Check the container and the inner equipment for damage at least once per month.
  • For containers stored for more than six months, open the door and inspect the interior to ensure there is no visible condensation.

Verify that the container and internal equipment are undamaged.

  • Inspect the system after powering it on and initiating startup procedures.
  • If required, request professional assistance for testing before installation.
  • For BESS systems stored for more than six months under the above conditions, a charge and discharge cycle shall be performed to bring the system SOC to 30% The storage period begins from the date of departure from the HyperStrong factory.

Battery Coolant Pipeline Network Description of Refrigerant Pipeline The refrigerant (R32) within the liquid cooling unit pipeline of closed system is used for cooling the load and flows sequentially through the plate heat exchanger, compressor, condenser, and expansion valve before returning to the condenser. In the plate heat exchanger, the refrigerant absorbs heat from the load (the glycol mixture in the battery liquid cooling pipeline) and vaporizes, simultaneously generating cooling capacity. The compressor draws in and compresses the gaseous refrigerant from the plate heat exchanger. The compressed high-temperature, highpressure refrigerant then enters the condenser, where it is cooled by a cooling medium (such as air). The condenser liquefies the refrigerant, releasing heat. The cooled refrigerant then passes through the expansion valve, where its pressure is reduced. The cooling medium returns to the plate heat exchanger, and the gas/liquid mixture vaporizes again, absorbing heat to achieve refrigeration.

Description of Cooling Medium Pipeline The pump draws the cooling medium (glycol mixture) from the battery cooling pipeline.

After being cooled by the refrigeration system, the cooling medium is then delivered to the load. The cooling medium with high-temperature enters the plate heat exchanger for heat exchange. The cooling medium that is cooled by the plate heat exchanger continues to circulate. This heat exchange process cycles continuously.

8 Emergency Operation And Accident Handling

The faults of the ESS primarily include battery faults, control system faults, equipment faults and physical damage. For common faults, please refer to the maintenance manual. This chapter will address the emergency faults, such as fire and electric leakage.

If there is any uncertainty about identification or handling of abnormal conditions, please contact the relevant technical or after-sales service department of the Company for professional technical support.

When contacting us, please provide the following information, which will help you root

the cause and clear the fault quickly and accurately:

  • Equipment serial number, production date and software version
  • Fault information and brief description
  • Photos of faulty parts
  • Information about the manufacturer, model, and configuration of the external devices connected to the energy storage system (e.g., transformers, upstream circuit breakers, and cable models).

8.1 Emergency Handling

If any of the following conditions (including but not limited to) occur, immediately stop using the ESS container and the adjacent battery container to prevent electric shock, fire, or explosion.

In Case of Fire:

In case of electrical equipment catches fire, please immediately disconnect the power supply and use dry powder or carbon dioxide fire extinguishers to extinguish the fire.

If there is an open flame outside the ESS container, please immediately evacuate from the ESS system and contact the local fire emergency services for assistance.

After emergency troubleshooting, stand and observe for a specific period. If no abnormality occurs, proceed to the next step of troubleshooting.

Typical ESS Emergencies and Anomalies:

  • Abnormal sound, spark, odor or other irregularities occurs during the operation or maintenance.
  • Smoke or fire in the container.
  • Expansion, rupture, melting, deformation of the battery pack.
  • External or internal short circuit.
  • Extreme weather conditions, such as rainstorms and flash floods.
  • Thermal runaway occurs during use, indicated by a battery temperature exceeding 85°C.
  • Structural damage that has not yet caused a severe accident.
  • The coolant of the chiller leaks.
  • The auxiliary power supply is abnormal or the communication is interrupted.
  • Damage to the main structure of the battery container or IP protection of the battery system.
  • Loose or burnt marks on electrical connections.
  • Insulation fault alarm. If triggered, stop using immediately and verify whether the alarm is false.

If there are any of the above situations occur (include but not limited to the above case), which may result in the ESS dysfunction, please contact professional maintenance team or technical support immediately of the Company.

If the situation is critical, please activate the contingency plan (refer to the maintenance manual), and notify first responders or fire services.

  • Before troubleshooting faults, replacing spare parts, or upgrading software, ensure that the DC and AC power supplies are disconnected. Use a multimeter to check that all devices are powered off. Ensure that the devices under maintenance cannot unexpectedly power on.

8.2 Emergency Operations

Power-off Process 1. Power off via remote control The BAMU sends a shutdown command to BMS after receiving the shutdown command from EMS. Once it is powered off, the system will restore to the initial state. The main circuit of the ESS container will be disconnected, which will depressurize the high voltage in the main circuit. The RUN indicator on the cabinet door is turned off after the system is powered off.

2. Power off via local control Open the door of the electrical compartment of the container, and click the shutdown command on the touch screen of the SCDC. Confirm that the contactor of the main circuit on the touch screen has been disconnected. The RUN indicator on the cabinet door is turned off after the system is powered off.

2. When the SCDC is in the remote-control mode, the control commands can be issued by the EMS. Please refer to related operation steps in the EMS manual.

  • Manual emergency stop shall not be performed in case of fire.
